# The midterm exam

## How the exam works

- **120 minutes.**
- **20 multiple-choice questions and 2 open ones**, with up to **300 words** for each written answer.
- **Wrong answers subtract.** Leaving one blank is not the same as getting it wrong.
- **Sat individually and in person.** Anything else counts as not sitting it at all.

## The voluntary questionnaire

Completely voluntary, and a real advantage in the exam. You write your own questions about the course material — and the good ones can end up on the paper. Writing a question means going back through the content and deciding what actually matters in it, which is most of the revision done already.

## Example: an open question

**Explain in detail the causes and symptoms of Simulator Sickness in virtual reality.** Describe the design strategies and training techniques that can be used to mitigate it and improve the user experience in VR, and mention a practical case in which Simulator Sickness was mitigated.

**An answer of the expected length:**

Simulator Sickness, also called cybersickness or motion sickness, is a set of symptoms users can experience in virtual reality: nausea, vomiting, dizziness, disorientation, headaches, eye strain, sweating and general discomfort. It can be caused by a sensory conflict between the visual information and the vestibular information responsible for balance and spatial orientation.

Design strategies to mitigate it include reducing that sensory conflict, limiting simulated movement, providing a wide field of view with a tunnelling vignette during action, and keeping a high framerate. Training techniques include gradual acclimatisation to VR, more natural interfaces, and training the vestibular system.

In one practical case, the movement inside the virtual environment was introduced deliberately by the designer as part of the experience, so that it matched the physical movement a rollercoaster was producing in the user’s own vestibular system.
